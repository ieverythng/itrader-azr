8.1 Target and source boundary

The realized fixed-panel weighted effect is descriptive and exactly computable. An interval concerns its proposed repeated-market/calendar sampling counterpart, conditional on the checkpoint pairs and locked selection design; it is not randomization over frozen market paths, training-seed inference, or an unbiased estimate over every 2020-2021 session. Generalization beyond selected windows requires a stated stochastic/design assumption. Metadata selection depending on availability/exposure can itself restrict that target.

Shao (2010), sections 2, 4 and 5 motivates mean-zero, unit-variance multipliers correlated at actual time distances, with a positive-semidefinite covariance kernel. Its regular-series results assume stationarity, moments and weak dependence with growing samples and bandwidth restrictions. Section 4 studies expanding lattice regions; section 5 uses random locations sampled independently from the process under density/domain conditions. Arbitrary-layout implementability is not a coverage theorem for our stratified sparse fixed panel. The adaptation below is our qualification proposal, not an application certified by that paper.

8.2 Fixed weights and centering candidate

For paired net differences d[k,s,a,h] from section 3, collapse dependent horizons and the two fixed pairs before primary uncertainty:

x[s,a] = (1/2) sum_k (1/4) sum_h d[k,s,a,h]
q[s,a] = 1 / (12*n_s)
theta_hat = sum_s sum_a q[s,a]*x[s,a]

m_hat[s] = (1/n_s) sum_a x[s,a]
e[s,a] = x[s,a] - m_hat[s]
c[u] = sum_{observed (s,a) on session u} q[s,a]*e[s,a]

Use exactly these Q24 weights, not pooled episode counts, per-bar weights or inverse-volatility weighting. No per-draw renormalization. Symbols with extra anchors retain total weight 1/12. Mean-centering does not change theta_hat; it changes the variance estimator and therefore requires explicit acceptance.

First centering candidate: symbol-specific anchor means, intended to retain fixed symbol heterogeneity while modeling within-symbol calendar variation. Some symbols have only two anchors: fitting those means can seriously underestimate variance, particularly with serial dependence. Applying n/(n-1), leave-one-out or another leverage correction is a separate candidate, not a universal repair. Pooled centering x-theta_hat instead can inject differences between fixed symbol means into sampling uncertainty. Register that alternative as a diagnostic, not select between them using terminal results.

If time-varying symbol means are allowed, neither stationary symbol centering nor arbitrary detrending is enough. With two observations per symbol, we cannot reliably infer that mean path from this panel. Synthetic oracle-known means may diagnose centering error but cannot become an operational estimator. No proposed centering can qualify arbitrary heterogeneous/nonstationary processes from these counts.

For pair-specific descriptive uncertainty, repeat the same reducer and centering separately for each k, but use the identical calendar multiplier draw. Do not bootstrap k or claim twice the independent calendar sample size.

8.3 Joint calendar law and PSD construction

Let u be the ordinal of each actual exchange session in the approved full calendar, retaining gaps even when no anchor is selected. Thus Monday after Friday is one session apart, while selected dates ten exchange sessions apart remain ten apart, not adjacent rows. UTC session identity, holidays, early closes and decision offsets remain metadata. This geometry is not outcome-derived regime labeling, and W varies over dates: it is not one constant multiplier for the entire panel. An elapsed-day alternative is a different distance law requiring separate declaration/calibration.

First kernel proposal, for a positive bandwidth b measured in exchange sessions:

K[u,v] = max(1 - abs(u-v)/b, 0)       # Bartlett
W_observed ~ Normal(0, K_observed)
Delta_star = sum_{observed sessions u} c[u]*W[u]
theta_star = theta_hat + Delta_star
V_hat = c' K_observed c

This kernel is PSD on session coordinates; restricting it to observed dates preserves PSD. Use a symmetric eigendecomposition square root so legitimate singular PSD matrices do not require Cholesky jitter. Predeclare floating-point symmetry/eigenvalue tolerances before implementation; roundoff-only correction must be bounded/logged, while materially negative eigenvalues fail. Do not silently project an invalid matrix to PSD or change b after seeing outcomes. The displayed zero outside the kernel's support is multiplier covariance, not a fabricated market return.

One W[u] is shared across all symbols, paired policies, horizons, checkpoint pairs and any registered contrasts on that session. This does not posit perfect correlation of returns: their joint residual contributions enter the same weighted sum. It avoids independently resampling contemporaneous market shocks. Do not add independent symbol/seed multipliers that break this law.

Retain each full maximal information/outcome interval [start-60,start+200). Overlapping horizons within an anchor remain attached. Cross-symbol intervals can share shocks despite different offsets; all same-session anchors receive the same multiplier, a deliberately coarse cluster approximation. Nonoverlapping intraday windows are not presumed independent, and disjoint sessions can remain serially dependent. If qualified metadata reveals cross-session windows, this session-only mapping must be redesigned before calibration. Sparse cross-date symbol overlap may leave common/idiosyncratic dependence poorly identifiable; a shared multiplier alone does not recover unobserved covariance.

No zero outcomes, imputed residuals or synthetic symbols on unobserved dates. Generating latent W on a full calendar is permissible, but only observed contributions enter the statistic. Missing outcomes invalidate the panel, not just that draw.

8.4 Bandwidth/support and interval proposals

Do not set b from 32 anchors, 128 anchor-horizon cases or two seeds as though these were independent sample counts. On qualified metadata, register a finite grid: a short-session candidate, candidates bridging occupied-date gaps, and a longer-persistence stress candidate. Exact values, selection rule and numerical tolerances must be accepted before simulation; no terminal-outcome bandwidth fit or significance-maximizing scan. Compact kernel support asserts negligible dependence beyond b, which must be challenged by longer-lived synthetic factors.

Report occupied-session count/span/gaps; symbol-session incidence; full-window overlap components; fixed Q24 weight per date/component and its concentration; kernel rank/spectrum; and the number/weight of temporal neighborhoods actually supported at each b. Kernel rank or many multiplier draws is not an effective sample-size certificate. Isolated dates make dependence unestimable; near-common multipliers can produce V_hat near zero because centered contributions sum to zero. Declare support/concentration/stability failure criteria before calibration. Do not erase dates, redraw the layout or enlarge b until a desired verdict appears.

First interval candidate: unstudentized basic multiplier interval in original net-return units; no invented sqrt(128) scaling. For conditional quantiles Q_p of Delta_star:

two-sided interval = [theta_hat-Q_(1-alpha/2), theta_hat-Q_(alpha/2)]
one-sided lower bound L = theta_hat-Q_(1-alpha)
primary superiority: H0 theta <= 0 versus theta > 0; reject only if L > 0

Alpha and coverage tolerances remain pending. Test the boundary null theta=0 and negative-null configurations. Gaussian linear multipliers yield a symmetric conditional root; they do not reproduce arbitrary finite-sample skew or heavy tails. Require nondegenerate V_hat; no zero-width interval on a constant or unsupported panel. Studentization requires a fully specified repeatable variance estimator and its own calibration, not dividing by an arbitrary SD. Fixed-b changes cannot borrow ordinary small-bandwidth reference laws: Shao and Politis. Calendar-block resampling remains a separately registered comparator if selected; repeat-until-all-symbols-appear rejection is not an innocuous implementation.

Next human question: should we use the following tolerance-qualified, independent synthetic confirmation gate for the nominal two-sided 95% interval, with a central equal-tailed construction as the first interval candidate?

The equal-tailed construction is a proposal: nominal two-sided 95% alone does not require balanced tails. For that candidate, target total noncoverage is 5%, with 2.5% in each tail. Under theta=0, declaring positive uplift from L>0 is one of those tails, not a new one-sided 5% test.

Support, calibration and existing software owners

Cited support / limit: Shao's DWB paper addresses stationary weak dependence and irregular sampling under stated asymptotic conditions. It motivates distance-aware treatment, not a theorem selecting our clock or validating this finite stratified panel.

Local inference: same-date symbols have distance zero under both clocks; the proposed interface keeps one synchronized date multiplier across symbols, horizons and fixed pairs, not an already implemented or qualified procedure. Resolve canonical trading date rather than grouping symbol-bearing session_id values as different dates. Sparse anchors change neighborhoods under each clock; unselected sessions are coordinate gaps, not zero observations. Neither a 505-session envelope, extra latent days nor more draws adds observed independent support. Qualified metadata must later bind date incidence, gaps, overlaps, fixed weight concentration, units and kernel PSD/rank. No layout matrix or support audit is computed here.

Keep the proposed interface inside existing evaluation owners: qualification / provenance binds reviewed coordinates/calendar identities; an uncertainty adapter receives frozen paired contributions/layout; the reporter retains Q24 reduction; disposition preserves valid descriptive estimates if inference cannot qualify. Legacy block uncertainty uses per-symbol positional-row blocks and is explicitly exploratory; relabelling it as a calendar method would be incorrect. No new evaluator, loader or source implementation is introduced.

A candidate K_ij=k(distance(i,j)/b) needs declared units and PSD checks. The same numeric b in sessions and days is not an equal-persistence comparison. Changing the clock changes neighborhoods and the method identity; later cross-clock DGP/inference cases must have frozen roles and units. Both mandatory families retain all required coverage/miss gates, including strong cases inside declared bounds. More clock/method checks affect the eventual simultaneous MC allocation; confidence, ranges/moments, bandwidths and budgets remain open. Section12.2's almost-common-multiplier centering collapse applies under either clock: a larger bandwidth is not automatically conservative.

Limitation: these windows end intraday and liquidate; they do not observe a held position's close-to-next-open return. Weekend news may influence later intraday outcomes, but its strength and decay clock are unverified assumptions. Neither elapsed days nor session distance can establish overnight/weekend portfolio risk from this endpoint. No prices/outcomes are inspected.

Proposed public oracles, not executed tests: ClockGapIdentity checks literal holiday/unselected-session distances and ordering invariance; SameDateCoupling maps distinct symbol sessions to one date coordinate; FixedTargetInvariant retains Q24 weights/point estimate under both clocks; ClosureAndDisposition keeps latent closed days out of RTH observations and rejects missing identities, unit conflicts or invalid covariance without deleting valid descriptive evidence.

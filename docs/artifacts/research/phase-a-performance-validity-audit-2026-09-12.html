Executive conclusion

The two historical PBT checkpoints are real and restorable. They contain trained SolverMLPFiLM policies and observation-filter state after roughly 0.9–1.0 million environment steps. The historical curves also show finite reward signals, non-collapsed training, and non-trivial action distributions. That is useful evidence that PPO can interact with the Phase A environment and optimize the shaped objective.

It is not evidence of economic performance. The metric named custom_metrics/episode_return_mean is the episode's accumulated optimization reward, while the metric named episode_return is the normalized reward component called pnl_delta. Neither is raw portfolio return. PBT selected on that shaped metric using the same data used for training, without a held-out evaluation environment. One selected checkpoint has an invalid PBT-mutated discount factor of gamma=1.188; the other has gamma=0.6272, whose discount half-life is about 1.49 steps despite 96–200 step tasks.

A deterministic, symbol-safe diagnostic replay of 288 episodes per checkpoint produced the following independent arithmetic-return measurements:

What is valid, and what is not

Supported claims

  1. The Phase A PPO/environment loop runs and produces finite optimization rewards.
  2. The MLP-FiLM checkpoints restore successfully under Ray 2.9.0.
  3. The historical policies are not a single constant-action collapse in their original training reports.
  4. The environment's reward has enough local signal for PPO to change its policy and value estimates.
  5. The current random/generated task path works without an LLM backend.

Unsupported claims

  1. The PBT score is not an economic episode return.
  2. The two winners are not held-out winners; they were selected and observed on the training file.
  3. The peak winner is not a valid PPO configuration because gamma > 1.
  4. The historical top-two comparison does not establish a statistically significant strategy difference. Its rows are training reports, not independent evaluation episodes.
  5. The available Parquet file is not three full years of duration. It spans 2023-07-05 through 2025-07-03: 729 calendar days across three calendar labels.
  6. Neither restored policy passes a modest 1 bp turnover-cost diagnostic.

The precise answer to “was the reward learnable?” is therefore: learnable as an optimizer objective, yes; validated as a proxy for economic performance, no.

Checkpoint replay protocol and result

The diagnostic evaluator restored each complete Algorithm checkpoint, including its observation filter. It then used explore=False on fixed family-00 tasks while preserving the historical task-embedding fields.

The 719,855-row dataset contains AAPL, AMZN, and MSFT. For every symbol it divided ordered rows into early/middle/late temporal thirds, then selected eight deterministic starts for each of four horizons. All 288 windows per policy were asserted to remain inside one symbol. Economic measurements were calculated independently of RewardEngine:

  • turnover from every target-position change;
  • terminal liquidation turnover;
  • net return at 1 bp per turnover unit;
  • arithmetic max drawdown;
  • matched long-only result.

The final winner's small positive gross mean disappears under costs. It is negative on all three symbols net of costs, and its late-segment mean is −0.1854%. The peak winner is negative gross and net. Both policies are overwhelmingly flat in deterministic inference. Ray 2.9-compatible PBT practice adopted

The current Ray guidance still recommends random initial hyperparameters, frequent checkpointing at perturbation boundaries, explicit mutation spaces, and replayable PBT policy schedules. Ray 2.9 supports burn_in_period, custom_explore_fn, log_config, and categorical list mutations, but it rejects duplicate metric ownership between the scheduler and tune.run. The repaired seam therefore gives the scheduler sole ownership of the PBT objective, aligns checkpoint and perturbation intervals, uses a burn-in, validates post-mutation configs, and records an independent history because Ray's Windows policy log is not durable here.

Primary references: Ray's PBT guide, PopulationBasedTraining API, and PB2 PPO example. Version-specific behavior was also confirmed against the installed Ray 2.9.0 signatures and source; later documentation was used as practice guidance, not as proof that newer APIs exist in 2.9.

Batch size is deliberately not mutated in this Phase A study. With training_iteration as the Ray 2.9 PBT clock, mutating it would compare members after unequal environment exposure. A later upgrade may instead use a timestep clock and a trainable/checkpoint contract designed around that unit.

Metric conflation

PSMetrics assigns accumulated reward to episode_return_mean and assigns the accumulated normalized pnl_delta reward component to episode_return. RewardEngine multiplies raw arithmetic PnL deltas by a dynamic ret_unit. A deterministic price probe produced raw arithmetic return 0.00377537 but the reported pnl_delta component was 3.78058, roughly 1,000 times larger. The ablation runner then labels and plots this field as “Episode Return Mean”, and train_solo_V2.py makes it the PBT selection objective.

The shaped reward is allowed to be scaled; the defect is presenting it as a return and using it as the only promotion score.

Data and split safety

The Parquet file is grouped contiguously by symbol, while itrader_env.py indexes the reset window and future returns without a symbol boundary. The theoretical fraction of invalid global starts is low—about 0.043% to 0.072% depending on horizon—but non-zero. In a 2,000-reset simulation per worker, a worker produced an AAPL→AMZN episode. Low frequency does not make the accounting correct.

A chronological split must be constructed by timestamp within each symbol, then purged so that neither the 60-row observation window nor the 96–200 row outcome horizon overlaps a neighboring split.

Task semantics

encoder.py encodes family, price_id, reward ID, horizon, dimensions, and seed % 997. samplers.py separately samples reward weights and start positions. Since reward parameters are not encoded, the same observation/task embedding can ask the solver to optimize different objectives. Since the seed is encoded and also controls start selection, the task representation leaks a location proxy.

For Phase A, either freeze reward parameters to one declared objective or introduce a versioned task encoding that represents every solver-relevant degree of freedom. Do not overload TaskSpec with broker, risk, or execution semantics.

Target design

The architecture should make optimization evidence and economic evidence distinct by construction.

TaskSpec + market split -> ITraderEnv -> EpisodeEvidence
                                 |             |
                                 v             +-> EvidenceWriter -> evaluator/capability report
                           RewardEngine
                                 |
                                 +-> optimization_reward + named components -> PPO/PBT

ITraderEnv remains the deep accounting module

ITraderEnv should continue to own market-window assembly, task-conditioned reset, action-to-position transitions, and episode accounting. At terminal step it should emit a public, versioned EpisodeEvidence record containing:

  • raw gross arithmetic return;
  • explicit cost schedule and net return;
  • turnover including terminal liquidation;
  • max drawdown and per-step count;
  • action counts;
  • symbol, start/end indices and timestamps;
  • split ID and dataset fingerprint;
  • task identity and canonical reward configuration;
  • optimization reward and finite named components.

This is a deeper seam than having callbacks scrape private environment state. Two consumers justify it immediately: the RLlib callback adapter and a standalone checkpoint evaluator.

RewardEngine remains the optimization module

RewardEngine should continue to own finite solver reward calculation. Its output should be named optimization_reward, never episode_return. Reward normalization and clipping must be recorded, but economic return must be computed independently from raw position and price transitions.

Evaluation stays outside TaskSpec

Introduce an EvaluationPlan or equivalent experiment contract for symbol-specific train/validation/test ranges, purge widths, cost scenarios, episode starts, baselines, seeds, and block-bootstrap units. These are experiment and evidence semantics, not broker or online risk semantics. MarketFramer, DecisionGate, RiskEngine, and Executor remain Phase C seams.

One evidence schema

The trainer, stable-metrics writer, checkpoint evaluator, and capability reporter should consume a single versioned record contract. Persist atomically to a run-owned evidence directory that Tune cannot truncate. A completed run is invalid if its manifest, resolved config, dataset hash, checkpoint index, or evaluation record is absent or empty.

Promotion gate for a Phase A winner

A checkpoint may be called a Phase A performance winner only when all gates pass:

  1. Provenance: non-empty manifest, commit, dirty-tree marker, resolved config, dataset SHA-256, Ray/Python versions, and checkpoint hash.
  2. Valid search: independently seeded population; diverse initial configs; every mutation validated; exploit events and lineage recorded.
  3. Strict data separation: per-symbol chronological train/validation/test splits with window/horizon purge; the test split unavailable to PBT and proposer updates.
  4. Frozen inference: restored training-only observation filter, explore=False, deterministic episode schedule.
  5. Truthful economics: gross return, net return under multiple cost assumptions, turnover, drawdown, exposure, and terminal liquidation.
  6. Baselines: flat, long-only, short-only, random policy, and the untrained policy under the identical schedule.
  7. Replication: multiple independent training seeds and block-aware uncertainty over non-overlapping time units.
  8. Stratification: symbol, time regime, horizon, task family, and reward configuration.
  9. Separation of concerns: optimization reward reported next to—but never named as—economic return.
  10. Capability report: generated from the same versioned episode-evidence schema and rejected on missing fields.

  • 00002 is the primary compatibility target because its gamma is finite and below 1, although it is too short-horizon for the stated tasks.

2026-09-14 checkpoint-boundary correction

The apparent iteration-40 discontinuity was a PBT exploit boundary: two losing members cloned one selected member and their evaluation turnover jumped from single digits to roughly 127. A frozen 16-task evaluation panel reproduced the same class of jump after an iteration-6 restore, ruling out changing evaluation tasks as the sole cause. A fresh restore of the selected checkpoint produced about 118 turnover even before another PPO update, while the score used to select it had about 2 turnover. The selection metric and restorable state were therefore not the same artifact.

Weight hashes proved that the selected and restored policies were identical. The discontinuity came from evaluation state: Ray 2.9 Algorithm.restore() copied the training policy configuration onto the evaluation policy and silently changed its explore flag from false to true. The restored policy was then sampled stochastically, producing roughly random-policy turnover. This is not negative grokking or a weight collapse.

Two secondary Ray 2.9 seams obscured the diagnosis. Algorithm.evaluate() returns the evaluation payload directly on this installation, while the first wrapper expected a nested evaluation key and silently retained the stale built-in result. RLlib also smooths over 100 episodes by default, which blended old and current policy evaluations instead of reporting the frozen 16-episode panel. Finally, Ray AIR consumes/truncates the directory passed to session.report on this Windows installation. The corrected contract is now:

  1. training remains start_idx_mode=stream_worker;
  2. evaluation uses one frozen task panel and deterministic starts;
  3. market windows use checkpoint-free market_relative_v1 features;
  4. PBT requires observation_filter=NoFilter in both train and evaluation;
  5. after every training/restore boundary, the evaluation policy is forced back to explore=False, learner weights are synchronized, and the direct Ray 2.9 evaluation payload replaces the built-in result;
  6. the metric-smoothing window equals the 16-episode frozen panel;
  7. each checkpoint is copied to stable, run-owned storage before Tune receives its checkpoint object; and
  8. the checkpoint used for selection must reproduce its score in a fresh process.

The final official two-member, six-iteration probe pbtA26_checkpoint_snapshot_probe_20260914_041234_pbt_checkpoint_snapshot exercised a non-terminal restore. At iteration 4, the selected source reported net -0.3539%, Sharpe -0.04580, and turnover 56.625. The clone's first post-restore update reported net -0.1016%, Sharpe -0.03783, and turnover 56.25; iteration 6 reported turnover 65.125. The spurious jump to roughly 127 is gone. A separate fresh process restored the durable source checkpoint and reproduced all three selection metrics exactly: net -0.0035387177838755394, Sharpe -0.04580387065499236, and turnover 56.625.

Next-P0 implementation round (staged 2026-09-14)

The late restore pattern is now fixed at its cause rather than merely documented. New trials report a target-owned cumulative sampled-environment-step clock in pbt_progress.json. Tune PBT uses pbt_progress_steps for burn-in and mutation cadence, checkpoints are emitted when that clock crosses the matching step boundary, and the target exposure budget no longer inherits a donor checkpoint's RLlib iteration. Historical report files remain readable through an iteration fallback, while new comparison plots align on exposure.

The earlier reward dominance is addressed with a separate, replay-safe objective, not by silently changing pnl_linear. net_return computes arithmetic PnL minus the configured cost in basis points for position turnover and terminal liquidation. A fixed scale of 1,000 supplies a practical PPO magnitude without changing the economic ordering; with cost_bps=1, summed episode reward divided by 1,000 equals independently derived 1 bp net return. This is a task/evaluation assumption and does not introduce broker execution into TaskSpec. PPO still discounts step rewards when gamma < 1, so equality of the undiscounted episode sum does not claim perfect equivalence to an undiscounted portfolio objective.

The next-P0 evidence seams now include:

  • frozen validation panels balanced over configured horizons;
  • deterministic flat, long-only, short-only, seeded random, and seeded untrained SolverMLPFiLM controls with per-episode receipts;
  • Ray 2.9 PPO columns for approximate KL, value explained variance, total loss, gradient norm, learning rate, and KL coefficient; clip fraction is explicitly unavailable rather than inferred;
  • a best-held-out cadence-checkpoint pointer that is marked as requiring fresh- process replay; and
  • optional terminal promotion gates relative to the strongest named baseline, requiring positive net return and positive held-out Sharpe by default.

Two official loadouts are staged: a 64-episode frozen-policy baseline panel and a bounded 20-update pnl_linear reference versus 40-update, four-member net_return PBT probe. Four-episode flat and untrained-policy smoke executions passed. Ray upgrade decision

Ray 2.9 should remain only as a locked reproduction environment. The current stable release is Ray 2.58.0, but upgrading in place would combine two independent changes: runtime/checkpoint behavior and RLlib's default-new API stack. The new stack replaces ModelV2 with RLModule, rollout workers with EnvRunners/Learners, and old trajectory/callback plumbing with Episodes and ConnectorV2. That is the right destination for iTrader, but it is not a patch-level dependency bump.

The repository currently has declaration drift that must be removed during the migration: pyproject.toml and itrader/environment.yml pin Ray 2.9.0, while itrader/constraints.txt pins 2.47.1 and itrader/infra/cluster.yaml installs 2.47. This is not a supported matrix. The locked 2.9 reproduction and the new 2.58 compatibility environment each need one authoritative, fully resolved lock.

Use a separate branch and environment with this sequence:

  1. lock the current Ray 2.9 environment and preserve one known reproduction;
  2. create a Ray 2.58.0/Python 3.11 compatibility environment;
  3. first disable the new API stack explicitly and prove dataset, task, reward, checkpoint, evaluation, and PBT parity on the current MLP baseline;
  4. then port SolverMLPFiLM to RLModule, metrics to RLlibCallback, rollout ownership to EnvRunner/Learner, and normalization to an explicit ConnectorV2 or the environment; and
  5. run the same fixed-panel checkpoint parity and short PBT acceptance loadout before any new long search.

Prefer Linux or WSL2 for overnight PBT: Ray documents Windows support as beta, with untested multi-node behavior, slower file operations, and path caveats. Unsloth is useful for LLM fine-tuning, not as a replacement for RLlib PPO, Tune PBT, or numerical-policy checkpoint semantics. It may later accelerate an LLM proposer experiment, but it cannot repair this solver-side state contract.

Primary references: Ray 2.58.0 release, RLlib new API migration, RLModule documentation, and Ray Windows installation caveats.

That 200-update run answered whether corrected held-out economic behavior emerged at roughly the historical training exposure. It did not, by itself, establish solver uplift. Matched flat/long/short/random/untrained controls and exploratory block/cost diagnostics have since run; independent seeds, genuinely new blocks, selection-aware inference, and the untouched final test remain mandatory for a performance claim.

Final judgment

The historical work was not meaningless: it established that the Phase A machinery can train a policy against a finite shaped reward. The mistake is in the strength and naming of the conclusion. The recorded “episode return” is not a portfolio return, the selected configurations were not constrained correctly, and the historical dataset was not held out. The corrected 200-update run, matched 120-update bridge, and raw-net 24k cadence study now score R0 against explicit controls. None passes economic promotion. The solver is active and reward-sensitive, and the raw-net challenger proves bounded positive economics can be learned on the selection panel; repeatable independent uplift over directional controls remains unproven.

The correct current status is:

Phase A economic measurement, checkpoint/evaluation parity, exposure-aligned reporting, frozen controls, and synchronous Ray-2.9 checkpoint/exploit/resume behavior are repaired and live-validated. PPO is active. The raw-net 24k run produced a replay-exact, 1--2 bp-positive transient with positive Sharpe and regime-varying actions, so economic learnability is supported on the frozen selection panel. It persisted for only two reports, its paired block interval versus short-only crossed zero, and no independent lineage reproduced it. Historical and current candidates remain unpromoted. P1 must establish independent new-block stability, selection-aware uncertainty, and per-step cost/Sharpe sensitivity before the sealed final test or any Phase B dependency on proven economic performance.

Phase A MLP-FiLM Economic-Performance Autoresearch

Date: 2026-09-19 Branch: agent/Watson-dev Scope: Phase A SolverMLPFiLM, PPO/GAE, synchronous PBT, environment-step scheduling, frozen validation controls, checkpoint replay, and economic evidence. Recurrent/LSTM solvers, live execution, broker semantics, and deterministic risk shells remain out of scope.

Decision standard

This study separates three claims that were previously easy to conflate:

  1. PPO activity: finite gradients, nonzero policy updates, value learning, and policy changes.
  2. Candidate economic learning: sustained positive 1 bp net return and Sharpe on a frozen validation panel, with non-flat behavior and a meaningful margin over matched controls.
  3. Validated economic performance: independent population replication, exact fresh-process replay, positive 2 bp repricing, block-aware uncertainty, and an untouched final-test result after selection is frozen.

Only the first claim was established before this loop. A Phase A checkpoint is now called a candidate when it has at least five consecutive evaluation reports with positive 1 bp net return and Sharpe and beats the strongest matched directional control on both metrics. The aspirational profound gate is ten such reports, fresh replay, positive 2 bp net return, a distinct policy hash and lineage, reproduction under an independent PBT population seed, and supportive block evidence. Directional action bias is diagnostic rather than disqualifying: only an effectively all-flat policy is an automatic action-based rejection. Regime-conditioned behavior is evaluated separately.

Architecture and measurement guardrails

  • TaskSpec remains the task contract.
  • ITraderEnv still owns task-conditioned reset, observation assembly, and reward context. It now exposes a narrow normalized override method for future resets; it is not a backtester or execution shell.
  • RewardEngine remains the only reward calculator.
  • The solver learns policy/value behavior. No broker, risk, or live-execution semantics moved into the solver or environment.
  • Training uses stream_worker, random/generated tasks, and no LLM backend.
  • Evaluation uses 32 deterministic validation episodes balanced across horizons 96, 128, 160, and 200. The final chronological test remains sealed.
  • Economic evidence is always reported independently at 1 bp per turnover unit, even when the optimization objective uses 0 or fractional basis points.
  • PBT burn-in, perturbation, checkpoints, and curricula use target-owned cumulative sampled environment steps. Restored RLlib iteration numbers do not control scheduling.

New seams implemented test-first

The loop added eight bounded seams:

  1. A strict step:value optimization-cost schedule, resolved from monotonic target-owned environment steps.
  2. Training-worker reward overrides that take effect on subsequent resets while leaving the frozen evaluation task panel unchanged.
  3. A separate pbt_population_seed, allowing new initialization/search randomness without changing validation tasks or starts.
  4. Durable promotion diagnostics: trailing passing-report counts, baseline-relative Sharpe, a near-all-flat veto, 0.5/1/2 bp repricing, paired block intervals, and ex-post bullish/bearish/sideways action summaries.
  5. An opt-in lexicographic PBT donor score: positive-net, positive-Sharpe, non-all-flat members form the eligible tier and are ranked by net return; when no member is eligible, ordinary net-return ordering is preserved. Directional action bias is never penalized by this score.
  6. A strict fail-closed donor mode that refuses to overwrite a lineage when the upper quantile contains no promotion-eligible checkpoint.
  7. Source-tree fingerprints and repository-owned, fsynced exploit/progress/ checkpoint evidence so dirty-code runs and zero-byte Tune state are visible.
  8. Explicit checkpoint recovery inputs for target-owned progress, frozen-panel seed, and checkpoint-consistent evaluation, plus a Ray-2.9-local synchronous scheduler adapter for stranded or orphan-staged paused trials.

Staged-cost arm

The schedule transitioned at the intended target-owned steps: 0 bp through 192k, then 0.1 bp, 0.25 bp at 240k, 0.5 bp at 288k, and 1 bp at 360k. Four configuration-changing PBT boundaries occurred. The best retained score was only +0.06941% net at the 0.1 bp stage; every terminal member was negative or effectively flat at 1 bp. The arm fails promotion.

The most likely explanation is timing rather than a broken curriculum seam. The discovery winner's durable improvement appeared after roughly 300k steps, whereas the staged arm introduced cost pressure at 192k and increasingly favored inactivity before the gross edge emerged. Future cost curricula should start from a retained gross-edge checkpoint or delay pressure until after an explicit edge gate; they should not repeat this early monotonic schedule.

Exact-1-bp arm

The exact-cost arm also fails. PBT propagated a checkpoint that briefly reached +0.10210% net and +0.01069 Sharpe with 75% flat actions, but it remained below matched short-only on both metrics. Every terminal member converged to roughly -0.065% to -0.081% net, non-positive Sharpe, and about 48--50% flat actions. Embedding the full evidence cost from step zero therefore neither found a stronger edge nor merely collapsed to 100% flat; it learned a weaker mixed policy whose early best checkpoint was still inferior to the trivial directional control.

Eligibility-aware 40-update probe

The replication exposed a contract mismatch: promotion required positive Sharpe and rejected effectively all-flat behavior, while PBT donors were ranked only by point net return. A test-first, opt-in selection metric now makes positive net return, positive Sharpe, and non-all-flat behavior lexicographic without changing reward, reported economics, or the default legacy PBT objective. Ineligible members remain in a finite lower tier ordered by net return; eligible members are still ordered by net return.

A four-member, 192k-step synchronous probe from the winner recipe exercised the new contract. Two genuine config changes occurred. Member 00003 generated two eligible reports; its best was +0.09902% net, +0.00691 Sharpe, 4.38% flat at 128,040 steps. Fail-closed replication and 24k cadence study

The eligibility-aware probe still allowed ordinary ordering when every member was ineligible. That was useful for backward compatibility but not strict promotion search: a negative donor could overwrite an independent lineage. The promotion scheduler now fails closed. A lower-quantile member may clone only an upper-quantile donor whose selection score is positive; otherwise the scheduler retains the member and continues training. Raw-net PBT remains available as an explicit diagnostic control.

Eligibility-aware 24k arm and recovery evidence

The same 314159 population was then rerun with only the synchronization and checkpoint cadence changed from 48k to 24k. There were no exploit events: member 00001 was eligible for two consecutive reports, peaking at 139,376 steps with +0.09433% net, +0.00453 Sharpe, and 92.16% / 6.75% / 1.09% short / flat / long, but no positive eligible donor existed at a synchronization decision that could improve the population. The other members produced no eligible reports.

Ray 2.9 then exposed two separate platform defects. First, synchronous PBT could leave the last paused member beyond a stale boundary while reporting an idle cluster. Second, after interruption, Tune's newest Windows experiment-state files could be truncated to zero bytes. Repository-owned metrics, progress, checkpoint indices, and immutable RLlib checkpoints remained intact. An explicit recovery arm restored member 00003 from its last stable checkpoint at 361,272 steps, seeded the target-owned clock at 73 updates, and reused evaluation seed 30260938. It completed at 386,008 steps with +0.07032% net, +0.00089 Sharpe, and 92.89% / 5.02% / 2.09% short / flat / long—again below short-only.

Automatic Tune resume is therefore not an evidence authority on this Ray-2.9/Windows stack. Recovery must name an immutable repository-owned checkpoint, its exact progress boundary, and the frozen evaluation seed. The training adapter now also releases a Ray 2.9 orphaned staged marker after a synchronous exploit; a four-member 30-update smoke crossed the previously deadlocked boundary and completed all members at approximately 144k--149k target-owned steps. This is deliberately localized in the PBT adapter rather than leaking Ray internals into the solver, environment, or task contract.

Memory-safe paused-donor verification

The run exposed two additional orchestration facts. CPU mode silently clamped a requested single rollout worker back to two, and a paused Ray 2.9 donor could have no Tune-owned checkpoint even though the durable checkpoint index already contained the exact boundary state. Test-first fixes now respect one worker, allow the local Ray node's declared CPUs to cap concurrent trial execution, recover a paused donor from the latest indexed checkpoint at or before its scheduler clock, record exploit/fallback/skip receipts, and suppress actor-local plotting when auto_plot_on_end is false.

The first memory-safe four-member diagnostic used one rollout worker per trial, declared four Ray CPUs, and therefore ran two trials at a time. All members reached the approximately 97k-step synchronization boundary without memory or storage pressure. Ray's scheduled donor save nevertheless resolved to None, so the scheduler emitted an explicit donor_checkpoint_unavailable skip. The run was stopped at that boundary rather than spending another 100k steps on a known missing-exploit path.

A test-first retry now replaces that empty Ray future with the latest durable checkpoint at or before the donor's trial-owned clock and repeats the exploit transaction. The 25-update verification recorded:

  • durable fallback for donor 00001 at 97,368 steps;
  • one real 00002 <- 00001 exploit at 97,384 / 97,368 steps;
  • mutation to lr=0.00435848, gamma=0.98, clip 0.2, minibatch 512, 20 SGD epochs, value clip 20, and value coefficient 0.5; and
  • successful target restoration and continuation through 121,584 steps.

That probe then localized a second Ray-2.9 tail defect: the paused upper donor was later released with another trial's transient Tune checkpoint. Three members reached update 25, while donor 00001 remained at its intact 97,368-step durable checkpoint. This is failure provenance, not a completed economic comparison.

The final adapter repair reattaches a paused upper donor's own stable checkpoint before Ray schedules it. Exploited lower members are not overwritten because Ray clears their scheduler-owned checkpoint before cloning. A separate terminal guard also prevents PBT from starting a new mutation after a member has reached the declared target exposure. The corresponding red/green regression suite is 75 tests across the scheduler, training config, and ablation runner.

Current interpretation

The repaired evidence no longer supports the earlier blanket statement that the Phase A reward was merely learnable in an optimizer-only sense. It now supports a stronger but still bounded result: the current MLP-FiLM PPO solver can discover policies on the validation selection panel that retain positive economics after 1–2 bp costs, beat the matched directional control, remain positive across all four horizons, and persist for two successive evaluations.

It does not yet prove validated economic performance. The two best policies share ancestry, the validation panel selected them, paired intervals are not selection-adjusted, and the final test remains sealed. Exact replay and regime-conditioned receipts passed, but two independent populations failed to reproduce the candidates. Independent replication and the final-test gate remain mandatory.

Storage remediation and deferred A/B

After this evidence pass, a storage incident audit found that a 77.94 GB WSL Node crash dump—not PBT artifacts alone—caused the abrupt C-drive exhaustion. Active ray_results and 37 experiment directories are now junctioned to the D NVMe, cold crash diagnostics are on E, and original report paths remain valid. See phase-a-storage-incident-audit-2026-09-19.md for byte counts, junction receipts, and the deferred WSL-distro move.

NVMe placement may improve Parquet startup, checkpoint copying, Ray metadata, and plot/report generation, but it is unlikely to multiply PPO throughput if the workers remain CPU-bound after the dataset is cached. Measure it rather than assuming it.

The ready A/B protocol is one short fixed-config run per storage target with the same commit, data hash, seed, workers, 32-task panel, update budget, checkpoint cadence, and no concurrent workload. Put Ray temporary files, results_root, stable checkpoints, and the Parquet copy on the selected target together. Run each target twice in alternating order and compare median time_this_iter_s, total wall time, checkpoint-boundary latency, and effective sampled environment steps per second. Keep the economic metrics out of the storage decision except as a determinism check. The active NVMe output root is now D:\iTRADER-offload\active; moving an active Ray run remains explicitly prohibited.

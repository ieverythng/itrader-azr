Retained Ray 2.58 optimizer and restore audit

Date: 2026-10-05. Read-only inspection; no training, replay, edits or deletion of historical artifacts. Ten terminal checkpoints plus three G0 checkpoints were inspected sequentially after verifying full checkpoint SHA-256 against each retained index. Policy files were approximately 2.18 MB each.

Findings and limits

All ten terminal checkpoints identify Ray 2.58.0 and persist native num_epochs=30, minibatch_size=128. Their declared configs supplied legacy aliases, not these native fields. All five exploited terminal targets have policy/optimizer settings differing from the intended algorithm configuration; their reported LR follows the intended value rather than saved Adam LR.

The unexploited causal member 00002 retains its expected lower LR, but has the same epoch mismatch and six self-resume entropy spikes. “Unexploited” does not mean “never restored.” This limits recipe and optimization-history claims; it does not invalidate the observed returns of the policies actually produced, nor prove that the defects caused every flat basin or transient gain.

The audit is a bounded sample, not certification of all past runs. Saved metadata is not an unavailable per-step optimizer trace. Do not retrospectively rewrite histories, infer corrected economic outcomes, or discard checkpoints.

Consequence for the next control

Preserve the old policies as historical observed outcomes. A fresh run must name its effective epoch/minibatch recipe and repaired schedule/restore behavior. For lowest-confound representation attribution, use explicit native 30/128 across the fresh R0/R4 pair and later R1 pair; call any intentional 20-epoch change a separate optimizer factor. Do not deliberately recreate entropy-reset spikes as normal training. This recommendation does not authorize training or claim a matched full-history reproduction.
